# Core papers

## Maestas, Mullen and Powell (2023)
Nicole Maestas, Kathleen J. Mullen and David Powell, **The Effect of Population Aging on Economic Growth, the Labor Force, and Productivity**, *American Economic Journal: Macroeconomics* 15(2): 306–332 (2023).

- [Working-paper PDF (NBER w22452)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22452/w22452.pdf)
- [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1257/mac.20190196)

**Read for:** measure of population aging (60+ population share), the projected-aging instrument, long differences, effect on GDP per capita, labor force/employment and labor productivity. Original unit of analysis is US states.

## Acemoglu and Restrepo (2017)
Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo, **Secular Stagnation? The Effect of Aging on Economic Growth in the Age of Automation**, *American Economic Review* 107(5): 174–179 (2017).

- [Working-paper PDF (NBER w23077)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23077/w23077.pdf)
- [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.p20171101)

**Read for:** cross-country evidence, aging measure (old versus prime-age workers), adoption of automation, and interpretation of cross-country aging-growth associations.

The PDFs are linked rather than redistributed. Before adding a PDF to this public repo, check the version's redistribution permissions.

## Literature note template
For each paper record:
- Estimand and dependent variable
- Units, sample, years and population
- Exact aging measure and units
- Empirical specification, fixed effects, covariates and standard errors
- Identification assumptions, exclusion restrictions and limitations
- Tables/figures to reproduce
- Differences from the proposed Europe/Africa application

**Open methodological issue:** A country-level shift-share/instrument design based on cohort forecasts is not automatically exogenous; migration, mortality, fertility and baseline economic conditions can affect the results.
